================================================================================
WHO APEX ABSENTIA IS FOR
And, just as importantly, who it is not for
================================================================================

Absentia does two things most encryption tools do not.

It keeps a secret that needs more than one piece to open, so that stealing your
computer and guessing your password is not enough.

And it can destroy that secret if you stop checking in.

That second part is unusual, and it is the reason this document exists. A tool
that can erase your own files is right for some people and actively wrong for
others, and knowing which you are matters more than any feature list.

Everything below is a description of a real situation. Find the one that sounds
like yours.


================================================================================
PART ONE: PEOPLE WHO HOLD SOMETHING THAT ISN'T THEIRS
================================================================================

If somebody trusted you with information, you are carrying a risk on their
behalf. They cannot check whether your laptop is encrypted. They just have to
hope.

JOURNALISTS

    You have a source. Their name, their messages, the documents they took a
    risk to hand you. If your notes are read, you are inconvenienced and they
    may lose their job, their liberty, or worse.

    Absentia helps in two ways. Split keys mean somebody who takes your laptop
    and works out your password still cannot open the file, because one of the
    pieces is on a USB key in a drawer at home. And if you are detained and
    cannot get to a computer for three days, the material destroys itself
    rather than waiting patiently for somebody to break it.

    The duress password matters here too. Somebody demanding you open your
    laptop sees a vault open onto ordinary files, and what actually mattered is
    already gone.

LAWYERS AND PARALEGALS

    Privileged client material, case files, settlement discussions, things
    told to you in confidence that could ruin somebody if repeated.

    A stolen laptop is a disclosure event with professional consequences. Split
    keys mean the theft alone is not enough, and the audit log gives you
    something to say about what was and was not accessed rather than having to
    assume the worst.

DOCTORS, THERAPISTS AND COUNSELORS

    Session notes. Diagnoses. The things people tell you they have told nobody
    else.

    You probably carry some of this on a laptop because the alternative is
    working only at the office. Encryption at rest is the minimum; a vault that
    also requires a second piece means a bag stolen from a car is not a breach
    of every patient you have ever seen.

CLERGY

    Confessions, pastoral conversations, family crises. In many traditions this
    is a duty you cannot be released from.

    The same reasoning as therapists, with the added point that you are
    unlikely to have an IT department.

HR AND WORKPLACE INVESTIGATORS

    Complaints, witness statements, investigation notes. People spoke to you
    because they believed it would stay contained.

    Leaks here cause real harm to people who took a risk by speaking up. A
    vault whose contents cannot be opened by a single password is a reasonable
    answer to "how do I know this won't get out".

ACCOUNTANTS AND FINANCIAL ADVISORS

    Client tax records, account numbers, identity documents. A lost laptop is
    somebody else's identity theft, not yours.


================================================================================
PART TWO: PEOPLE FOR WHOM BEING FOUND OUT IS DANGEROUS
================================================================================

For the people in this section, the deadman switch is not a curiosity. It is
the whole point.

PEOPLE DOCUMENTING ABUSE

    You are keeping a record: photographs, messages, dates, a diary of what has
    happened. It is evidence, and you need it. It is also the single most
    dangerous thing in your home if it is found.

    Absentia lets that record exist without existing obviously. The vault gives
    nothing away about what it holds. A duress password opens it onto something
    innocuous if you are made to unlock it. And if something happens to you and
    you cannot check in, it does not sit there waiting to be discovered.

    Please read the warnings about accidental destruction carefully. Set a
    generous deadline. This is a situation where losing your own evidence would
    be its own disaster.

WHISTLEBLOWERS

    You have taken something you were not supposed to take, for reasons you
    believe are right. You may be one of very few people who could have taken
    it.

    A search of your home and machines is a realistic outcome. Split keys mean
    the search is not enough. The deadman switch means that if you are arrested
    and held, the material does not sit waiting.

ACTIVISTS AND ORGANIZERS

    Membership lists, meeting notes, plans, contact details. In many places
    these endanger everybody on them and not only you.

    The custodian feature is designed for exactly this: a piece of the key held
    by somebody in another country, released only while you keep checking in.
    Seizing your machine gains nothing, and neither does waiting.

RESEARCHERS AND AID WORKERS IN HOSTILE PLACES

    Interview recordings, names of people who talked to you, location data.
    The people who spoke to you did so at a risk they may not fully have
    understood.

    Border crossings are the specific problem. A duress password gives you
    something to hand over.

PEOPLE CROSSING BORDERS

    Not only in dramatic circumstances. Many countries can require you to
    unlock a device, and refusing has consequences.

    A duress password means you can comply. The officer sees a vault open onto
    holiday photographs and a shopping list.

    Be clear-eyed: this is not magic. If somebody images your drive and
    examines it later, a mostly empty container raises a question. It works
    against somebody watching your screen, which is the common case.


================================================================================
PART THREE: PEOPLE PLANNING FOR NOT BEING HERE
================================================================================

This is the use the name comes from. Absentia means judgment passed on somebody
who is not present.

PEOPLE WITH A SERIOUS DIAGNOSIS

    You are thinking clearly now about a time when you may not be able to.
    Some things you want your family to have. Some things you very much do not.

    Absentia handles the second kind. Letters that were never sent. A diary.
    Photographs. Records of something you dealt with alone and do not want
    discovered while people are grieving.

    Check in weekly for as long as you are able. When you stop, it goes.

ANYBODY WITH SOMETHING THEY WANT TO TAKE WITH THEM

    You do not need a diagnosis for this. Most people who have lived a while
    have something they would rather not have read aloud after a funeral:
    correspondence, a record of a difficult period, something about somebody
    else that was never theirs to share.

    The ordinary options are bad. Deleting it feels like destroying the only
    record. Keeping it means somebody finds it. A vault that quietly closes
    itself after you stop checking in is a genuine third option.

PEOPLE IN DANGEROUS WORK

    Soldiers, correspondents, people who go to places with a real chance of not
    returning. The material you carry should not outlive you and fall into
    whoever picks it up.

EXECUTORS AND THE ORGANIZED

    A slightly different shape: you want some things destroyed and other things
    found. Absentia handles the destruction half well. For the delivery half,
    use it alongside a proper will and a lawyer. It does not deliver anything
    to anybody.


================================================================================
PART FOUR: ORDINARY PEOPLE WITH ORDINARY SECRETS
================================================================================

None of the above needs to apply for this to be useful.

SOMEBODY WITH A PASSWORD FILE

    Nearly everybody has one, and most of them are a text file or a note on a
    phone. A vault is strictly better and not much harder.

    Split keys are the real benefit here: somebody who takes your laptop and
    works out your login still does not get everything else you own.

SMALL BUSINESS OWNERS

    Customer records, supplier pricing, your own accounts, the details that
    would let somebody impersonate your business. You have no IT department and
    no security budget.

FAMILIES WITH SENSITIVE PAPERWORK

    Immigration documents, adoption records, medical histories, the paperwork
    of a difficult divorce. Things you need to keep and do not want lying
    around.

PEOPLE WHO SIMPLY DISLIKE BEING READ

    That is a sufficient reason. You do not have to justify wanting a private
    place on your own computer.


================================================================================
WHO THIS IS NOT FOR
================================================================================

This part is as important as everything above. The wrong tool confidently
applied is worse than no tool.

ANYBODY WHO CANNOT AFFORD TO LOSE THE FILES

    If the contents are irreplaceable and losing them would be a disaster,
    think very hard before enabling the deadman switch, and possibly do not use
    a vault with one at all.

    It will do what you told it. If you are in hospital for a fortnight, or
    travelling somewhere without a computer, or simply forget, it destroys your
    files and nobody can undo it. That is not a malfunction.

    If you want the files protected but definitely kept, use full-disk
    encryption and leave the deadman switch off. That is a perfectly reasonable
    way to use this.

ANYBODY WHO FORGETS THINGS

    Said kindly, because it applies to most of us some of the time. A deadman
    switch is a commitment to a routine. If you know you will not keep it, the
    tool is not wrong for you but the feature is.

ANYBODY WHO NEEDS AUDITED SOFTWARE TODAY

    Absentia has not been independently reviewed by cryptographers. That is
    stated everywhere in this project because it is the most important thing
    about it. If you are handling material where a professional review is a
    requirement, use something that has had one. VeraCrypt and Cryptomator both
    have.

ANYBODY WHO NEEDS TO HIDE THAT A VAULT EXISTS

    Absentia does not attempt this. The vault file is visible, and so are the
    files beside it. What is hidden is the contents, and whether a duress
    password exists. If the existence of an encrypted file is itself
    incriminating where you are, you need a tool built for deniability.

ANYBODY EXPECTING PROTECTION FROM A COMPROMISED COMPUTER

    If somebody is already inside your machine while a vault is open, they can
    read what you can read. No vault fixes that.

ANYBODY WHO HAS NOT READ "BEFORE THE VAULT"

    Not a joke. The file existed on your disk before it went into the vault,
    and traces of it are probably still there. docs/BEFORE-THE-VAULT.txt
    explains what to do about that, and for most people it matters more than
    anything in this document.


================================================================================
THE SHORT VERSION
================================================================================

Use Absentia if you are holding something that would harm somebody if it were
read, and you want it to be safe now and gone if you stop being around to look
after it.

Do not use the deadman switch if losing the files would be worse than somebody
reading them.

And turn on full-disk encryption regardless of what you decide about this
program. It does more for you than any single thing here.
